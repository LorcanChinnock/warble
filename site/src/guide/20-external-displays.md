Some Macs and most displays have no notch, so Warble can draw one of its own at the top centre of the screen. It works the same way as the real one: hover over it or click it to open it.

## Where it shows

- **On a MacBook with a notch,** Warble uses the notch while the lid is open. When you close the lid and use an external display, the notch moves to the display that has the menu bar.
- **On a Mac without a notch,** such as a Mac mini or an older MacBook Air, Warble draws one on the display that has the menu bar.

Other displays don't show a notch. Drag the menu bar to another display in System Settings › Displays and the notch follows it.

## What it looks like

A real notch has the camera in the middle. The notch Warble draws is a plain black shape with the same album art and moving bars either side, and with nothing playing it isn't drawn at all, unless you choose otherwise below.

## Choose when it shows

In Settings, **Notch on external displays** sets when a notch that Warble draws is there:

- **Only when playing.** The first choice. The notch appears when something plays and goes when the song has been paused for a while.
- **Always.** After that, an empty black notch stays at the top of the display, with nothing in it. It doesn't react to the pointer, and clicks go through to whatever is under it.
- **Off.** Warble draws no notch of its own, so a display without a notch shows nothing. Settings is still in the menu bar icon's menu, so you can turn it back on.

This only applies to a notch Warble draws, never your MacBook's own.

## Show the title in the middle

A real notch has the camera there, but a notch Warble draws has nothing in the middle. Turn on **Show the title in a virtual notch** in Settings and it shows the title of what's playing, with the artist after it when both fit. A title that's too long scrolls slowly past in a loop while the song plays, and stops when you pause. With **Reduce motion** on, it stays still and fades out at its end instead. You can also point at the notch to see the title in the player. Tags such as "(Remastered 2009)" or "(feat. …)" are left out so more of the title fits, and a paused song's title turns grey.

It's off at first, and it only applies to a notch Warble draws, never your MacBook's own.

## Full-screen apps

When an app is full screen, it has the whole display to itself, so the notch on that display hides until you leave full screen.
