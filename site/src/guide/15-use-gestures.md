title: Use gestures
summary: Scroll over the notch to change the volume, and turn on swiping left or right to skip songs and dragging the album art into other apps.

The notch answers a few gestures, so you can change what's playing without opening it, or share it with another app.

## Change the volume

Scroll up or down over the notch, with two fingers on a trackpad or with your mouse's wheel. Up is louder, and down is quieter. It works even with the notch closed, and it's on from the start.

## Swipe to skip

Swiping is off at first.

1. Right-click the notch, or click the Warble icon in the menu bar, and choose **Settings…**.
2. Choose **Notch**.
3. Turn on **Swipe over the notch to skip**.

Then point at the notch and swipe:

- **Left** with two fingers skips to the next song.
- **Right** goes back.

One swipe skips one song, however far it goes. On a Mac with a Force Touch trackpad, you feel a light tick as it skips. Swiping works whether the notch is closed or open, and does nothing while nothing is playing.

Left and right mean the way your fingers move, whichever scroll direction you've set in System Settings › Trackpad.

> [!NOTE]
> Swiping needs a trackpad, or a Magic Mouse, where you swipe with one finger. A mouse with a wheel can't swipe sideways, so use the skip buttons in the player instead.

> [!TIP]
> Warble decides which way you're going from the first moment of the gesture. Scroll mostly up or down and it changes the volume; swipe mostly sideways and it skips, without touching the volume.

## Drag the album art out

Dragging the art is off at first.

1. Right-click the notch, or click the Warble icon in the menu bar, and choose **Settings…**.
2. Choose **Notch**.
3. Turn on **Drag the album art out of the player**.

Then share what's playing:

1. Point at the notch to open the player.
2. Press on the album art and, keeping the button held, drag it out of the notch.
3. Drop it where you want it, such as a message, a note, a document or a folder.

The player stays open while you drag. What you get depends on where you drop it:

- **A folder or the desktop** gets the picture as a file, named after the song.
- **A message, a note or a document** gets the picture.
- **A text box or a browser's address bar** gets a link to the song, when the app playing gives one.

A click on the art, without dragging, still brings the app that's playing to the front.

> [!NOTE]
> Most apps give Warble the album art but no link. Spotify gives one only when Warble follows it the backup way, which it does only while its usual way of seeing what's playing isn't working. Without a link, dropping the art in a text box does nothing.
