# The notch

Read this before changing the panel, its states and cards, hover, displays, toasts, scrolling, shortcuts or the keyboard. Back to the [architecture index](../../CLAUDE.md#architecture).

## Surfaces and displays

`NotchController` (`Sources/Warble/Notch`) keeps a `NotchSurface` for each camera housing that `VirtualNotch.housings` (WarbleModel) returns: the built-in display's notch, and a virtual one at the top centre of each display without a notch whose `VirtualNotchRule` asks for one. By default only the main display gets one, and only while no display has a real notch (the lid is closed, or the Mac has none). Rules are stored per display UUID under `PreferenceKey.virtualNotch`, and the menu bar menu and Settings › General both set them (`VirtualNotchPicker`). Each surface puts its `NotchPanel` over its housing in a `NotchState`, at the frame `NotchLayout` works out, while `PlayerModel` holds what's playing once for all of them. The controller only turns displays, from the `DisplayProvider` port (`Displays`), and their rules into surfaces and dispatches `.housingsChanged`, again whenever either changes. In `Store.onChange` it copies the state into `PlayerModel` and `DoctorModel` and has each surface draw what `AppState` says for it (`NotchSurface.apply(_:)`, then `update`). Turning mirroring on or off sends several screen changes while the window server rebuilds the displays, and it moves windows to fit each half-built arrangement. `NotchSurface.draw` skips a layout whose frame hasn't changed, so `NotchSurface.replace()` puts the panel back where its layout has it, on each layout and whenever `NotchPanel` reports a move (`onDisplaced`).

## Full-screen apps

The panel joins every Space, full-screen ones too, since a nonactivating panel at its level joins them whatever its collection behaviour asks. A full-screen app hides the notch on its display instead: `FullScreenSpaces` (`Sources/Warble/Displays`) reads each display's current Space type from the window server (`CGSCopyManagedDisplaySpaces`, looked up with `dlsym`, so without it no display counts as full screen), and `NotchController` dispatches `.fullScreenChanged` on `NSWorkspace.activeSpaceDidChangeNotification` and each layout, never on a timer. `AppState.Surface.isFullScreen` makes `notchState` Hidden, so the notch draws nothing and takes no hover, and the panel lets clicks through (`ignoresMouseEvents`).

## The title in a virtual notch

With `PreferenceKey.virtualTitle` on (it starts on, the one extra that does, since the middle of a virtual notch is otherwise empty), a resting virtual notch draws the title of what's playing in its middle, where a real one has the camera: `AppState.titleInNotch(_:)` is true for a virtual surface that isn't full screen while something plays, and `NotchSurface.apply(_:)` copies it to `NotchModel.showsTitle`. `TrackTitle` (WarbleModel, `Notch/`) cuts the tags a name collects ("- Remastered 2009", "(feat. …)", "[Live at …]", "(Official Video)") and picks "Title — Artist", the title or the title faded from widths `VirtualTitle` (`Sources/Warble/Notch/Views`) measures, so the notch stays `VirtualNotch.width` wide. A faded title scrolls to its end once for each track, in Core Animation (`NotchMotion.titleScroll`), never loops and never runs with Reduce Motion; `NotchModel.scrolledTitle` keeps it from scrolling again each time the notch closes over it. The text is a `CATextLayer`, since AppKit draws only the visible part of a text field, which would leave the end of a scrolled title missing. Such a surface's `notchState` leaves out the track-change toast, since the title scrolling says the same.

## Geometry

macOS reports the housing to the half point, so its edges and height can fall on half points. AppKit snaps windows to whole points, so the panel's window grows out to whole points on both sides (`NotchLayout.window(for:)`) and draws its shape centred, at the housing's exact width and height. The resting corners and wings follow the housing's height (`NotchLayout.cornerRadius`, `wing`), so they stay the same physical size at every display scale; `Tests/WarbleModelTests/Notch/MeasuredHousings.swift` holds what a 15-inch MacBook Air reports at each scale.

## Hover and dismissal

`HoverIntent` (WarbleModel) decides when the pointer opens a notch into Expanded, after `expandDelay` or at once on a click, and closes it again: it takes timestamped events and returns the next deadline instead of running timers. Each notch's lives in `AppState.surfaces`, which is `Notches` (WarbleModel, `Notch/`), a `Feature` like the rest: it handles the actions about the notches themselves, reads the other features' parts through `SharedState` (the toast, the Doctor's "!" and the demo's step, which it draws ahead of anything else), and works out `notchState`, `card` and `answersPointer` from them, so its deadline joins the store's one timer. It also settles itself (`Notches.settle` starts a hover over when a notch stops answering and shows the player again after one closes) and asks for the click, Escape and volume effects once each when their reason changes (`Notches.follow`); the settle pass in `reduce` keeps only what couples features, such as an open card dismissing the toast and the pointer holding off Idle. A surface dispatches `.pointer` from a tracking area over only the camera housing while resting and the whole panel once open, and `.hover(_, .click)` for a click on the panel, which also closes any other open notch. Only while a notch is open does `reduce` ask for `.watchClicksOutside` (a global click monitor in `PlatformEffects`) and, with the pointer on it, `.takeEscape` (a temporary `HotkeyCenter` key through the `HotkeyRegistrar` port, so no permission is needed); both dispatch `.dismiss`.

## Morphing between states

Between states the shape morphs on one spring while the content cross-fades; with Reduce Motion the shape changes at once and only the content fades. The panel grows before a morph and shrinks after it, so nothing is clipped.

## Expanded: controls and volume

Expanded holds the playback controls. They dispatch `.command`, and `reduce` shows play/pause, a seek and the shuffle and repeat modes at once (`PlaybackState.playing(_:at:)`, `seeked(to:at:)`) and asks for `.send`; the app's next report replaces that guess. A podcast or audiobook, which `SpokenWord` (WarbleModel) tells from `PlaybackState.mediaType` or the app, has back 15 and forward 30 seconds in place of previous and next while `AppState.skipsBySeconds` says so: only with `PreferenceKey.podcastSkips` on (it starts off), and never for a live stream. They dispatch `.skip(by:)`, which `reduce` turns into a seek from the position now, kept within the track. Warble has no speed control: the adapter can't set a chosen speed (its `speed` only steps the app's own list), and #143 was dropped. `SystemVolume` (WarbleCore) reads and sets the default output's volume through Core Audio. `reduce` asks for `.followVolume`, which listens for its changes, only while Expanded shows the player's card, and scrolling over the panel in any state dispatches `.scrolled` with the volume read just before, as `VolumeScroll` (WarbleModel) works out.

## Previews

To see the notch without playing anything, launch with `open build/WarbleDev.app --args -notchPreviewState resting`, which shows a preview track instead of reading the adapter, and hover or click it. Add `-notchPreviewVirtual YES` to draw the virtual notch instead, on a Mac that has a notch.

## The context menu

A right-click or Control-click on any notch, in any state, opens `NotchMenu`: the app it follows, Settings, updates and Quit, so Warble can be reached with its menu bar icon off. `NotchPanel.sendEvent` takes the click before SwiftUI, and while the menu is open `HoverIntent` keeps a resting notch resting and `reduce` lets go of Escape, so the key closes the menu.

## Toasts

`Toast` shows a short message near the bottom of the screen under the pointer. Notices that slide out of the notch, such as a track change, are another thing: `ToastQueue` (WarbleModel) decides which one shows and for how long, with no timers of its own. With `PreferenceKey.trackChangeToast` on (it starts off), `reduce` posts a `TrackToast` (WarbleModel) for each new track while no notch is open, and each resting notch draws it as `NotchState.toast`: the wings with the title and artist in a strip below, `NotchLayout.toastDrop` deep. Only the housing takes the hover, as at rest. Opening a notch, playback stopping or turning the setting off dismisses it. In the `resting` preview, next and previous change the preview track, so a shortcut for Next track shows the toast.

## Shortcuts and the keyboard

`HotkeyCenter` registers the global shortcuts of `WarbleAction`, none of which has one by default. A press dispatches `.perform(action)`, which does what the matching button does. `WarbleAction.openNotch` opens the notch on the pointer's display with the keyboard in it (`HoverEvent.keyboard`, which pins it open). Only then does `NotchPanel` take key, and Warble comes to the front, since VoiceOver and Tab follow only the app in front. Warble has no menu bar of its own, and `NotchSurface` gives the app that was in front its place back when the notch closes, unless a click has moved on. Notch buttons use `.buttonStyle(.notch)`, so Space or Return presses the one with the focus ring. Keys no control used reach `NotchPanel.keyDown`, and `NotchKey` (WarbleModel) says what each does: Space plays or pauses, ← and → seek 10 seconds, ⇧← and ⇧→ skip, ↑ and ↓ change the volume, Esc closes.
