# Live waveform

Read this before changing the waveform, the audio tap or what the play-state bars draw. Back to the [architecture index](../../CLAUDE.md#architecture).

Settings › Notch › Move the bars with the sound (`PreferenceKey.waveform`, off at first) makes the four bars beside the notch follow the Mac's audio instead of looping. `WaveformState` (WarbleModel, `Waveform/`) holds the setting, and `AppState.showsWaveform` is the setting and not Reduce Motion (`NotchController` and `AppEnvironment` fold that in, since it's an AppKit value). The settle pass at the end of `reduce` asks for `.captureAudio(true)` only while `AppState.drawsBars` holds: something is playing and not about to be hidden, no "!" has taken the bars' place, and a notch is Resting, or showing a toast or a lyric line, which keep the bars. It asks for `.captureAudio(false)` the moment that stops being so (a pause, Idle, a card opening, a full-screen app, the setting going off), so macOS never sees Warble recording while the bars aren't on screen, and with the setting off it's never asked. Each answer is asked once per change, like `.listenForCommands`. `EffectRunner` starts and stops the `AudioTap` port, and hands each frame to `EffectRunner.spectrum`, which `AppEnvironment` points at `SpectrumFeed` (`Sources/Warble/Notch`). The frames, about 15 a second, never pass through `reduce`: they would put work on the hover path for something only the bars draw. `SpectrumFeed` hops them to the main actor for the `BarsView`s that listen, and each smooths its own with `SpectrumBars` (WarbleModel: a dB scale, quick attack, slow release). `NotchRender.showsWaveform` is `AppState.capturesAudio`, so the bars follow the sound only while the tap runs, and loop otherwise.

`ProcessTap` (WarbleCore, `Waveform/`) is the real port: a `CATapDescription` of everything the Mac plays, unmuted and private, read through a private aggregate device clocked by the default output, and an IOProc that mixes the channels to mono and gives `SpectrumAnalyzer` the samples. The analyzer keeps 2048 samples, works out a Hann-windowed vDSP FFT every 1/15 s, and takes the biggest tone in each of the four bands in `SpectrumBars.bandEdges`, where a full-scale tone is 1. `ProcessTap` builds the IOProc block outside the main actor: made in a main-actor method it takes the actor's isolation and traps on the audio queue. It follows the default output (the aggregate device is clocked by the output it started with) by starting again, and releases the IOProc, the aggregate device and the tap in `stop`.

macOS asks to record the first time the tap starts, with `NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription` from `Resources/Info.plist` (set there, since the build-setting route is reported to drop it; check a built app with `plutil -p`). There's no public way to ask ahead, so the setting's footer says so. When the tap can't start, `ProcessTap` calls its failure callback, the runner dispatches `.audioCaptureDenied` (a callback from a tap that has since been stopped is ignored), `reduce` turns the waveform off, and `.turnOffWaveform` (`PlatformEffects`) clears the setting and sets `PreferenceKey.waveformRefused`, which Settings reads to say why. Turning the setting on clears it and tries again.

The FFT for a frame has a budget in [`docs/performance.md`](../performance.md). Measured with a tone playing through the demo player (`docs/demo/`), the waveform costs about 0.4% of a core (0.21 to 0.28 s of CPU a minute), inside the playing-at-rest budget of 0.5%, against 0.0% with the setting off. What it costs is mostly the notch's redraw for each frame, so the rate is kept low (15 a second, with `SpectrumBars`' attack and release set to match), a bar moves only in whole points (half-point steps cost 0.28 to 0.42 s a minute), and nothing is committed on a frame where no bar moved. The IOProc mixes the channels with vDSP. `make perf-app` checks this budget on a real tap: it needs the system audio permission for WarbleDev.
